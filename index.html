📝 What We Did

As we boarded the plane, the call went out for any duty-free purchases to be picked up at the gate before boarding the plane. Once we boarded the plane, we found our comfortable seats that were on the right with a window seat. The flight was long; however, entertainment was available and dinner and breakfast were served. We arrived in Hong Kong at 4:30 am local time May 14th.
The sky was dark and the humidity was high.

Once off the plane, we took a long walk to get to security and customs after picking up our baggage (these arrived intact thankfully). The heat and humidity was just a foreshadowing of what the whole day was going to be like in Hong Kong. After that, we found a 7/11 where we got some coffee, hoping to try out our WeChat and Alipay apps that we had downloaded on our phones. Unfortunately, they didn't work, presumably because we were not yet in mainland China. Luckily, we were able to get train tickets and a subway ticket (Octopus Pass) for Hong Kong. We also exchanged some Hong Kong dollars from the ATM.
I was worried because I went through every credit card I had twice before I finally got one to work. We couldn't figure out the WeChat which we were told would work. We were not able to receive the text verification code to finish the final steps of setting up WeChat for making the purchases.
We bought an Octopus card that said on it "China Tea Union," and said that it was good in mainland China as well as Hong Kong. However, as we would later learn, that actually wasn't the case. So we bought high-speed Airport Express tickets on the metro to go on that special train into Hong Kong, as well as to travel around Hong Kong with the Octopus card. Our plan was to explore Hong Kong while waiting to check into the hotel.

Once we arrived by train to Kowloon Island, we got off and walked to the main train station from where the subway stops. We were hoping to scout out the procedure as the next morning we would take a high-speed bullet train to Chongqing. We needed to know where to go for check-in and the procedure for customs into mainland China. Everything seemed to be relatively straightforward in that we would have a line to stand in for "assistance required" in the morning.
Our hotel was within walking distance from the train station (which Bruce carefully planned out to make the morning easier on us all).
Once we arrived at the hotel, we dropped off our bags, changed into some fresh clothing, and began our day of Hong Kong exploring.
Our first stop was the Kowloon Park behind the hotel which was full of beautiful flower beds, fountains, shaded seating areas, and very few people since it was still early in the morning.

Tsim Sha Tsui, station on one end of Kowloon Park

As we continued walking, we found a main street that led to a famous place called Chungking Mansions. The Chungking Mansions were evidently through a dark alleyway that was a market. We stayed in this area briefly and exchanged Hong Kong dollars for Chinese RMB. We then took the Subway Red Line to mainland Hong Kong to see the four-story Brandy Melville (Anneliese previously worked at the Scottsdale location) located near the Times Square shopping district in Hong Kong. We were near the Tsim Sha Tsui shopping district, but most of the shops were closed because it was still early morning.

I'll take just a moment to say that as we were looking for Brandy Melville, we went into the Times Square building mall, which is 17 stories high. The main eight stories are the mall and the other levels have restaurants and businesses. This very large mall was spectacular and filled with all the high-end designer brands. We saw groups of young primary students, boys and girls, on field trips through the mall; their teachers showing them the Times Square Mall. We eventually got to the Brandy Melville store and Anneliese bought a "Hong Kong" shirt.

It was then time for lunch at the Jardine Street area market. We found a lunch place that served dumplings. We ate a variety of food, such as a shrimp dumpling soup, a beef bun, and a few other items. The steamed buns were light and fluffy. Our server was abrupt and somewhat put-out that our translator app was not cued up quickly enough. We followed her recommendation as to what we should order, but she passed the order pad to Bruce to fill out. It all turned out well enough. After our lunch it began to rain, so we took the subway back to Kowloon Island and our hotel. Once we checked in, because it was such a hot and humid day, we took showers and rested up. We were planning to go out again, but Anneliese felt sick so we had to go back and rest for the night. Bruce did venture out to bring take-out to the room from Jollibee; a long walk to find some food for Lois and Anneliese. The city comes alive at night.

The first night in Hong Kong, Bruce did not sleep very well, but Anneliese and Lois slept very well this first night. (I think Bruce was a little concerned about making the train early the next morning.)
